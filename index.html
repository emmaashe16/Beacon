Alarm

Whistle plays the international distress signal: six blasts a minute. Three blasts is also widely understood as “help”.

Turn off silent mode and turn the volume up. Sound carries further than shouting and saves your voice.

Field guide

Basic first aid and survival steps. Call for help first whenever you can.

CPR (adult)
  1. Check for danger, then response. Shout and tap the shoulders.
  2. No normal breathing? Call 112 and put the phone on speaker.
  3. Heel of hand on the centre of the chest, other hand on top.
  4. Push hard and fast: 5–6 cm deep, 100–120 a minute.
  5. If trained: 30 compressions, then 2 rescue breaths. If not, keep compressing.
  6. Use a defibrillator (AED) as soon as one arrives.
Push on every beat.
Severe bleeding
  1. Press hard on the wound with a cloth or your hand.
  2. Keep pressure on for at least 10 minutes. Don't lift to check.
  3. If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top.
  4. Limb wound still bleeding heavily? Apply a tourniquet 5–7 cm above it and note the time.
  5. Keep them warm and lying down.
Choking
  1. Ask them to cough.
  2. Up to 5 sharp back blows between the shoulder blades.
  3. Up to 5 abdominal thrusts: fist above the navel, pull in and up.
  4. Repeat back blows and thrusts. If they go unresponsive, call 112 and start CPR.
Hypothermia

Signs: shivering, slurred speech, clumsiness, confusion. Shivering stopping is a bad sign.

  • Get out of wind and wet. Insulate from the ground.
  • Swap wet clothes for dry. Cover the head.
  • Warm the core first: chest, armpits, groin.
  • Warm sweet drinks if they can swallow. No alcohol.
  • Handle gently. Don't rub limbs.
Burns
  • Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes.
  • Remove rings and watches before swelling starts.
  • Cover loosely with cling film or a clean plastic bag.
  • No ice, butter or creams.
Fractures & sprains
  • Don't try to straighten it. Support it in the position found.
  • Splint with a stick, rolled jacket or trekking pole, padded.
  • Check fingers or toes stay warm and pink after tying.
Rule of threes

Roughly how long a person survives without:

  • 3 minutes without air
  • 3 hours without shelter in harsh weather
  • 3 days without water
  • 3 weeks without food

Prioritise in that order: breathing, shelter and warmth, water, then food.

Signalling for rescue
  • Groups of three mean distress: three fires, three whistle blasts, three flashes.
  • Ground-to-air: a large V means “need help”, an X means “need medical help”. Make lines 3 m long or more.
  • Bright colours against the background: spread orange or red gear in the open.
  • To a helicopter, both arms raised in a Y means “yes, we need help”.
Water
  • Prefer flowing water from upstream of people and animals.
  • Boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 m).
  • Filter through cloth first if it's murky.
Lost? STOP
  • Stop. Stay put. Walking usually makes it worse.
  • Think. When did you last know where you were?
  • Observe. Landmarks, weather, daylight left, your supplies.
  • Plan. Shelter early, signal, and conserve phone battery.
